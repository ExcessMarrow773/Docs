# HW #4
- **British-French Wars**
	- The Seven Years' War started in 1756 and lasted for either 7 or 9 years, depending on who you ask
	- American Indians allied with the French, making it also be called the French and Indian War
	- The British won in 1763, consolidating control of North America
	- Colonies became more confident in their ability to stand up for their interests
	
- **Colonial Indepenence**
	- The British tried to collect more taxes from the colonies. The colonists then proceeded to be more self-sufficient
	- They were emboldened by the Great Enlightenment to call for greater self-governance.
	- Clashing views led them to found a new nation
	
- **Articles of Confederation and the Constitution**
	- Inspired by republican ideals of the American Revolution, the country was governed by the Articles of Confederation
	- Twas weak, like ***really*** weak; they replaced it with the Constitution
	- The Constitution made a stronger federal government, but kept certain powers for the states.
	- The Bill of Rights added protections for basic liberties

- **Conflicting Views of Government**
	-  George Washington was the first president
	- Leaders of the new government argued over everything
		- Economy
		- Individual rights
		- Foreign affairs
		- Relations with Native Americans
		- Roles of federal and state governments
	- Two political parties emerged by the end of Washington's eight years in office
		- Democratic-Republicans
			- argued for stronger state gov.
		- Federalists
			- argued for stronger federal gov.
		- In the Election of 1800, the Democratic-Republicans defeated the Federalists; they peacefully transferred power
		- By 1820, the Federalists disappeared!
			- Their ideas stuck around though

- **Changes in Economics, Politics, and Culture**
	- Immigrants continued to arrive in the new country
	- Folks caused conflicts with the Native Americans, whose lands they were migrating into
	- The British, French, and Spanish, who were also there, claiming Native American land, were also a problem.
	- The United States defended its land and sea borders to protect its interests

	- People began to form their own cultures
	- The United States declared independence in 1776 and ratified the Constitution in 1788; only after 1800 would it be nationally recognized

# Seven Year's War
## **Empires at War: 1689-1763**
- In the late 17th century, some wars broke out involving Great Britain, France, and Spain.
- Often involved other Europeans and natives of India and North America
	
## **The First Three Wars: 1689-1748**

- Named after the British monarch whose reign they occurred under
	- King William's War: 1689-1697
		- The British launched expeditions to capture Quebec from the French, but failed
		- American Indians were supported by the French while burning British frontier settlements
	- Queen Anne's War: 1702-1713
		- British had more success
		- taking Nova Scotia from France
		- got trading rights in Spanish America
	- King George's War: 1744-1748
		- James Oglethorpe led a colonial army in Georgia that repulsed Spanish attacks
		- New Englanders took Louisbourg, a major French fortress on Cape Breton Island that controlled access to the St. Lawrence River
		- Britain returned Louisbourg to the French in the peace treaty in exchange for political and economic gains in India
		- New Englanders were big mad about the loss of the fort
## **The Decisive Conflict**
-
	- In the first three wars, the European powers didn't think it was worth it sending regular troops to America.
	- relied on "amateur" colonial forces.
	- Also, most of the fighting was in Europe
		- but by 1754, after the fourth conflict began, conditions had changed
	- GB and France realized how good the colonies were at giving them free money (raw goods)
	- only 60,000-is settlers lived in the French colonies, but they worked with the Native Americans for ***(French with the) #FUR***
	- British colonies were more populated, with about 1.2 million people
		- Produced grain, fish, tobacco, lumber, and more

	- The final war in this quartet was the **Seven Years' War** (see above for more information)

- **Beginning of the War**
	- British POV
		- The French provoked the war by building forts in the Ohio River Valley
	- The reason (partly) why the French did this was to STOP THE GROWTH OF THE BRITISH COLONIES
	- Hoping to stop the French from finishing Fort Duquesne, colloquially known as Pittsburgh, and winning control of the Ohio River Valley. 
	- The governor of Virginia sent a small militia under the command of George Washington(!); they had a good small victory, and proceeded to surrender to some Frenchmen (they were superior) and their American Indian allies. 
		- This was on July 3, 1754; not sure why the text gave this specific information
		- --
	- The British were having a bad time at the start. 
	- 1755: another expedition from Virginia, led by **Edward Braddock**
		- ended in a big, bad defeat
		- more than 2,000 British regulars (civies?) and colonial troops
		- The French repulsed a British invasion of French Canada that started in 1756

- **The Albany Plan of Union**
	- The British government called for representatives from a few colonies to meet in Albany, New York, in 1754.
	- Seven colonies adopted an agreement the **Albany Plan of Union** (<small>Written and directed by Benjamin Franklin</small>) that allowed an intercolonial government, 
	- Also a system for recruiting troops and collecting taxes from the various colonies for their own good (everybody's defense)
	-	The colonies were too concerned about keeping their taxation powers to accept the plan, though, so it never actually happened.
	-	The Congress was significant, cause it set a precedent for later congresses in the 1770's

- **British Victory**
	- William Pitt (British prime minister) concentrated the military on conquering Canada
	- They completed this objective after
		- Retaking Louisbourg in 1758
		- Quebec being surrendered to General James Wolfe in 1759
		-  Montreal being taken in 1760
	- After these victories, European powers negotiated a peace treaty (**Peace of Paris**) in 1763
	- Britain "*acquired*" French Canada and Spanish Florida
	-  France, feeling bad for Spain, gave up its huge territory west of the Mississippi known as Louisiana
	- French power in this continent pretty much ended here
- **Immediate Effects of the War**
	- Was a turning point for everybody
		- Gave Great Britain unchallenged supremacy among Europeans in North America
		- Challenged the autonomy of many American Indians
		- Established Britain as the dominant naval power in the world
		- The American colonies no longer faced the threat of concerted attacks from the French, Spanish, and their American Indian allies
	- 'Twas a change in how the British and the colonists viewed each other
	
- **The British View of the War**
	- Came away from the war with a low opinion of colonists' military abilities
	- Thought the American militia was poorly trained and disorderly
	- Noted that some colonies did not contribute troops or money to the war effort.
	- Convinced the colonists were unable and unwilling to defend the vastly expanded British empire
- **The Colonial View of the War**
	- Were quite proud of themselves in all four wars
	- developed confidence that they could provide for their own defense
	- Not impressed with the British troops or leadership, as the methods of warfare used were badly suited for the densely wooded terrain of eastern America

## Reorganization of the British Empire
-	- Used to, the British were pretty lax about their control over the colonies, and didn't enforce their Navigation Acts regulating trade.
		- This is called **Salutary Neglect**
		- This was also abandoned when they adopted more forceful policies for taking control of their colonies
	- All of the wars (the last one especially) were very expensive.
		- They also thought they had to keep a large military force to guard the colonies
	- The colonists did not like the heavy taxes the government created to fund the wars, and pressure was building to reduce them
	- **King George III** and the Whigs, the dominant political party at the time, wanted the colonies to pay more to maintain the British empire

- **Pontiac's Rebellion**: 1763
	- First major test of British policy
	- Chief Pontiac led an attack against colonial settlements on the western front
	- They were not happy about the fast westward-moving European settlers
	- They also did not like how the British did not offer gifts, as the French did
	- The American Indians that Pontiac led destroyed forts and settlements all the way from New York to Virginia.
	- The British then sent British troops, rather than colonial forces

- **Proclamation of 1763**
	- The British government issued a proclamation to prohibit colonists from settling west of the Appalachian Mountains
	- They hoped this would prevent hostilities with American Indians `...the colonists will remember that...`
		- The colonists did not like that
		- they thought they would get *some* sort of reward from the Seven Years' War
		- Thousands did not care and went westward past the line
- **Growing British-Colonial Tensions**
	- Divergent views on the war and changes in British imperial policies provided the reasons for conflict between Great Britain and its North American colonies

# (No) Taxation Without Representation
## British Actions and Colonial Reactions 
- The acts above were, from Britain's point of view, justified as a fair, proper method of protecting its colonial empire, as well as making the colonies pay for the protection
- A main issue that divided the British and colonists was representation
	- Colonists said they had no way to directly elect representatives to ** Parliament **, and as such had no voice in the choices made there
	- The British responded by saying the colonists had virtual representation in the government, like all other British citizens. 
## New Revenues and Regulations
- During the first two years of peace, King George Wii's treasury minister and prime minister pushed through Parliament three measures that made the colonies start to think the British were plotting to remove more of their liberties
	-	## The Sugar Act: 1764
		- Also known as the Revenue Act of 1764
		- Placed taxes on sugar not from Britain
		- A partner law also enforced the Navigation Acts more
	- ## The Quartering Act: 1765
		- Made the Colonists give food and quarters to the British soldiers who were stationed in the colonies
	- ## The Stamp Act: 1765
		- Britain needed more money for the military forces in the colonies
		- They then put taxes on stamps and made it so you *had* to put stamps on most printed paper
	- Reaction to the Stamp Act
		- **Patrick Henry**, a Virginia lawyer, stood up in the House of Burgesses to demand the government recognize the rights of all citizens
		- **James Otis**, in Massachusetts, started a call for action among the colonies to protest the Stamp Act
		- Representatives from nine colonies met in New York in 1765 to make the **Stamp Act Congress**
	-  With the formation of the **Sons and Daughters of Liberty**, things took a more violent turn
	- **Economic Pressure**
		- Boycotts against British imports were the best way to protest the new taxes
		- became fashionable to not buy anything British
		- Instaid of buying it from Britain, colonial women would just make it himself!
	- **Declaratory Act: 1766**
		- Grenville was replaced with another prime minister, and Parliament voted to repeal the Stamp Act, once the news reached the colonies, people rejoiced!
		- They failed to notice that they had replaced it with this act, which let them make taxes and laws for whatever reason
