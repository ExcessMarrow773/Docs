﻿# 11/3/26

- ## How did the reform movements of the 1840s change US society?
	- **Jacksonian Democracy**
		- after Andrew Jackson's presidency, there is a growing focus on the power of the poor man (aka the common man)
		- Universal White mail suffrage
			- If the poor white man can vote, then I should be able to vote too!" - Everybody else
		- **Market revolution**
			- The Market Revolution mad way for many advances in technology (steamboats, railroads, telegram, cotton gin)
			- These advancements led to economic growth and the rise of a middle class
			- encouraged people to grow their individual wealth

- ## What was the Second Great Awakening?
	- **A resurgence of spiritual and moral individualism**
	- Emphasized the **natural goodness** of people and **the choice for people to live holy lives**
	- People began to focus on what was morally right in society
		- *"If we all (society) become more moral, then we can all go to heaven!"*
		- *"Better yet, we can bring God's kingdom to Earth by making it a better place!"*
	- This need for society to more moral, along with the effects of political and economic shifts in the US, leads to many **reform movements** in the 1830s-1840s

- ### The Power of Self
	- Jacksonioan Democracy: More political independence
	-  Market Revolution: More economic independence
	- Second Great Awakening: More Spiritual/Moral independence

- ### Reform Movements:
	1. **Abolition Movement**- Calls for the end of slavery
	2. **Temperance Movement** - stop drinking; causes crime and violence
	3. **Women's Rights Movement** - women want the right to vote and to be independent -> opportunities to be educated & to work
	4. **Public Education** - EVERYONE deserves the right to education
	5. **Prison Reform** - prisons are actually doing the exact opposite of helping and have terrible conditions

- ## Public Education
	- Only **private** education really existed before this movement. So, if you could not afford to seek education, you were not educated 
	- Few free colleges existed, but they required those who enrolled to already know how to **read** and **write**
	- Reformers pushed for **free** elementary education across the US
	- Leader: **Horace Mann**

- ## Prison Reform
	- Prisons are unorganized and dangerous
		- Prisoners were often beaten, restrained, and neglected
	- Focuses on providing **mental institutions** for those who need mental health support, and a **juvenile system** to keep children separate from the normal prison population
	- Both groups experienced abuse, so this movement focused on creating sewerage spaces for them
	- Leader **Dorothea Dix**
