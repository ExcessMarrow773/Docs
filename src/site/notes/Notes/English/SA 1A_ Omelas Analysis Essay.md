In *The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas,* Ursula K. Le Guin uses symbolism to offer a way of thinking about the moral dilemmas of our modern world by making us think about the things that we normally willfully ignore, as well as building multiple worlds that could be, but ultimately denying them for things that go against the rules of Omelas.

 In this story, the community does not talk to the child, and by doing so, is able to keep its suffering hidden away, not seen or even thought about. LeGuin writes “The rules are strict and absolute; there may not even be a kind word said to the child.” (paragraph eleven) This sentence, emphasized by being its own paragraph, shows how important not speaking to the child is. In paragraph eight, she writes “...Sometimes speaks ‘… I will be good\!’ They never answer”. This emphasizes that if they talked to the child, they would establish a connection, making it harder to throw away the guilt. Together, these sentences show that acknowledging the child's suffering would only make it harder to hide it away, as well as giving us a connection with today's world that people will often not talk about, or willfully ignore, such as homelessness and sweatshops. 

Paragraph three has this: “Given such a description such as this… …One might look next for the king… …But there was no king…”, saying how you might expect something unbelievable, something you wouldn't expect in your world, she uses this to say there is no control over others, nothing to harm others. It also brings us, “Or they may have none of that: it doesn't matter”, which gives you an expectation that even if they have all these wondrous things, this story doesn't rely on them, only the concept of the child,  revealed later in the story. These pieces from paragraph three tell us things that could be, things that don't matter, but also things that most definitely are not. 

Like those who walk away from Omelas, the people who cannot live with the knowledge that there is a child suffering in a cellar closet, there are people in our society who cannot live with “The child in the cellar”, like homelessness or sweatshops, who do not every day ignore things that make their day harder, that make them think about what they could do, the people who walk away are the people who would help, the people who would fight for what they think is right.
