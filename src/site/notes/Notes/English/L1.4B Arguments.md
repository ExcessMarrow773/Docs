# Terms

- Claim
	- A defendable statement about the world that is either true or false
		- EXAMPLE: "I have never liked pickles"

	- Types of claims
		1. Claims of **fact** (i.e. Statement)
			- Polar ice caps are melting. The defendant was found not guilty. America is a democracy.
		2. Claims of **value** (i.e. Judgement)
			- Climate change is a serious problem. The jury made the right call. America is a great nation.
		3. Claims of **policy** (i.e. prescription)
			- We must limit dependence on fossil fuels. The prosecutor should appeal. We must save American democracy. 
- Evidence
	- A really broad term
	- A response to believe why a claim is true
		- NOTE: evidence usually appears as a claim
		- EXAMPLE: "75% of the world likes pickles"
	- Types of claims
		1. **"Facts"**: statistics, observation, information, et.
			-	validity/credibility based on authority (i.e. the source of information)
		2. **Reasoning**: Analogy, syllogism, analysis, et.
			- Validity/credibility based on logic (i.e. the thought process of speaker + audience) & relevance (i.e. the connection to the claim) & relevance (i.e. the connection to the claim)
			- NOTE: can't have one without the other
- The rhetorical situation
	- **author**: Who is making the argument?
		- what their POV and/of their stake is
	- **audience**: who is being addressed?
		- who the speaker needs to persuade
	- **purpose**: what do they want to accomplish?
		- what outcome they are hoping will result
	- **context**: why is the argument being made?
		- what event(s)/cause led up to the argument
